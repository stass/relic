# Bound query factoring

This document covers a query transformation within the Magic Sets rewrite.
For an eligible recursive predicate, it replaces the propagation of complete
answer tuples through recursive calls with propagation of requested states.
Base rules turn the reached states into answers. The original requested
binding accompanies every state, so calls from different origins remain
separate. The output is ordinary compiled rules for the existing analysis,
planner, evaluator and storage engine.

Related: [architecture](architecture.md#magic-sets),
[Magic Sets](design-magic.md), [planning](design-planning.md),
[evaluation](design-eval.md#the-semi-naive-fixed-point),
[storage](design-storage.md), [testing](design-testing.md),
[implementation plan](../docs/todo-benchmark-performance.md#3-factor-eligible-bound-recursion).

## Model

A call's adornment partitions a predicate's columns into a **state**, the
bound columns, and an **output**, the free columns. Both vectors retain
their original column order; their columns need not be adjacent. An
eligible predicate has the relational equation

```text
P = B union R P
P = R* B
```

Here `B(state, output)` is the union of its base rules and stored facts.
`R(state, successor)` is the union of its recursive rules' transitions.
`R*` includes the identity transition. `R` and `B` are fixed relations
defined over complete inputs; they need not be the same relation. They are
mathematical descriptions, not additional materialized relations.

Let `Q` be the set of state tuples the callers request. The generated
relations are:

| Relation | Meaning | Columns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `magic$p$alpha` | Requested original bindings, generated by ordinary callers | State |
| `walk$p$alpha` | A state reached from one requested origin through zero or more transitions | Origin, current state |
| `p$alpha` | Answers paired with the original requested binding | Original predicate columns: origin in bound positions, output in free positions |

The origin is the whole bound tuple, not a call identifier or just its
first column. Two requests for the same tuple share work. Different tuples
do not share an unlabelled set of answers. A constant query uses the same
representation as calls whose bindings come from other relations.

```text
query and original component schedule
    -> complete inputs materialized by the ordinary Magic boundary
    -> adornment search: certify a state/output separation or use ordinary Magic
    -> free-call subsumption determines the final retained adornments
    -> draft requested origins, state propagation and base answers
    -> ordinary stratification, planning, fixed points and query execution
```

The single-binding reduction is described in section 2 of Naughton,
Ramakrishnan, Sagiv and Ullman,
[Efficient Evaluation of Right-, Left-, and Multi-Linear Rules](https://sigmodrecord.org/publications/sigmodRecord/8906/pdfs/66926.66948.pdf).
The origin columns here extend the construction to correlated requests.
The proof below covers that extension and the exact eligibility contract;
the paper's broader classes are not eligibility rules for this transform.

## Invariants

* **Certification covers an entire adornment.** Every defining rule fits
  one relational equation before any factored rule or predicate is published.
* **Outputs pass through recursion unchanged.** The output variables
  occur only in the recursive head and matching recursive-call positions.
  Transition predicates cannot observe or change them.
* **Origin and current state have different roles.** Only the current
  state changes during propagation. Answers use the origin in their bound
  columns, never the final state's values.
* **Demand is not an answer.** A base rule or a stored source fact justifies
  every answer, including when the origin itself is reached.
* **Complete reads stay complete.** Factoring neither restricts a complete
  predicate nor materializes another dependency to make it eligible.
* **Search and draft share one decision.** A certified adornment is drafted
  from its certificate; an ordinary one uses its recorded planner walks.
  Drafting does not rerun eligibility or discover another call graph.
* **Generated rules use the ordinary publication funnel.** Safety, slot
  numbering, structural deduplication, attribution and charging apply before
  the rule table reaches the evaluator.
* **The original program remains available.** Unsupported calls, complete
  evaluation and rewrite-limit fallback use the established paths.

## Eligibility as a separation proof

Eligibility belongs to `(source predicate, bound-column mask)`, within
one query rewrite. It depends on the compiled rules and the fixed complete
set, not cardinality estimates, predicate spelling, worker count or data
values. A recognizer returns a complete certificate or ordinary rewriting;
allocation failure and exhausted rewrite work remain separate outcomes.

The source is a recursive component containing exactly one predicate `p`.
The adornment binds at least one column. A free call follows ordinary
subsumption. Every source rule has a body consisting only of positive
relational atoms. A nonrecursive rule reads only complete predicates. A
recursive rule contains exactly one occurrence of `p`, and all its other
atoms read complete predicates.

For each recursive rule, gather its head terms in bound-column order as
`S`, its head terms in free-column order as `Y`, and the recursive call's
bound terms as `T`. The certificate establishes the form

```prolog
p(S, Y) :- G(S, T, L), p(T, Y).
```

This notation groups columns for exposition; generated atoms preserve
the source's actual positions. `L` denotes existential local variables.
The source's recursive atom may occur anywhere in the written body.
The conjunction is positive, so the equation does not depend on that
written order or on the ordinary planner's preferred nesting.

The separation check establishes three properties:

1. Each term of `Y` is a distinct variable, repeated in exactly the same
   free column of the recursive call. It has no occurrence in `S`, `T`
   or `G`. If the adornment is fully bound, this vector is empty and the
   property is vacuous.
2. Each variable in `T` is supplied by `S` or a positive atom of `G`.
   Successor terms can be variables or constants; an unbound variable or
   wildcard in a successor column does not supply a transition head.
3. Every term and variable needed by the generated rules fits the existing
   compiled-rule representation after the origin columns are added.

These properties make `R` independent of the output. State terms can
contain constants, repeated variables and permutations: the generated
demand literal preserves those constraints. Wildcards remain legal in
complete input atoms where they are existential. Multiple recursive rules
contribute a union of transitions, not a sequence chosen by the recognizer.

Base rules have no output-separation restriction. Their heads can contain
constants or repeated variables, and their bodies can join several complete
inputs. They define arbitrary `B` under the positive relational fragment.
A source base store contributes the existing virtual rule over `p$base`.
No facts are expanded into generated rules or enumerated during recognition.

An expression, comparison, assignment, negation, aggregate, additional
recursive occurrence, mutually recursive member, or demanded external
predicate fails this certificate. The ordinary rewrite handles that
adornment. Restricting bodies to positive atoms preserves the relational
proof without changing where computations or diagnostics occur. No list
of supposedly harmless expression functions is part of eligibility.

The recognizer checks source rules, before generated guards and helper
predicates exist. An arbitrary guarded recursive program is not identified
with transitive closure by its names or its superficial shape. In particular,
the planner's guarded regression that depends on the demanded derived
predicate `reach` remains outside this certificate.

### Representation bounds

With `m` bound columns, `walk` has arity `2m`. This must fit
`RELIC_MAX_ARITY` (32); the answer retains the source arity. The uniform
origin representation therefore admits at most sixteen bound columns.
Wider calls use ordinary Magic even when a particular query supplies only
constants. No generated predicate is interned before this check.

For each generated rule, count the distinct source slots actually copied,
plus the `m` fresh origin slots. That total must fit `RELIC_MAX_VARIABLES`
(64). The demand seed has only `m` slots, each repeated in its head.
An output slot removed with the recursive atom is not counted in a
transition rule. Base rules can retain every source slot, so their bound
is checked independently. A failure declines the whole certificate.

All slot checks precede mask shifts and use of the fixed renumbering arrays.
A representable final rule does not authorize an intermediate slot beyond
the array: compact the copied source slots before assigning fresh origin
slots. The language's arity and slot limits are not increased.

## Generated rules

The caller's ordinary Magic prefix emits `magic$p$alpha(A)` and reads
`p$alpha` as before. For a factored adornment, the recursive source calls
are represented by transitions and do not generate more requests for `p`.

One seed rule starts a walk for every requested origin:

```prolog
walk$p$alpha(A, A) :- magic$p$alpha(A).
```

Each certified recursive rule contributes:

```prolog
walk$p$alpha(A, T) :- walk$p$alpha(A, S), G(S, T, L).
```

Each base rule `p(S, Y) :- H(S, Y, L)` contributes:

```prolog
p$alpha(A, Y) :- walk$p$alpha(A, S), H(S, Y, L).
```

The base head's actual terms form `S` and `Y`, so constants and equalities
in that head remain constraints on the walk and on the answer. Fill the
answer's original bound positions from `A`; copy the original free head
terms into its remaining positions. This projection is driven by the
adornment mask, not an assumption that bound columns come first.

The virtual base-store rule uses this same base construction:

```prolog
p$alpha(A, Y) :- walk$p$alpha(A, S), p$base(S, Y).
```

`A`, `S` and `Y` denote vectors in these schemas. They do not introduce
tuple-valued language terms, a new operator or a second evaluator.

### A single-source closure

For `path(X, Z) :- edge(X, Y), path(Y, Z)` with base rule
`path(X, Y) :- edge(X, Y)`, the query `path(a, Z)?` generates:

```prolog
magic$path$bf(a).
walk$path$bf(A, A) :- magic$path$bf(A).
walk$path$bf(A, Y) :- walk$path$bf(A, X), edge(X, Y).
path$bf(A, Y) :- walk$path$bf(A, X), edge(X, Y).
```

The rewritten query still reads `path$bf(a, Z)`. Only `(a, current)`
pairs occur in `walk`; reaching `b` does not cause the rewrite to store
every path starting at `b`. The transition and answer rule have different
heads and roles even when their bodies coincide. The ordinary evaluator
runs both, and its set storage removes repeated tuples.

### Positive length and different base relations

The seed `(a, a)` is demand, not a path. An isolated `a` with no applicable
base rule yields no answer. When `B = R = edge`, answers are `edge+`:
zero transitions followed by a base edge gives length one, and a cycle
can legitimately make `a` an answer to its own query.

For a distinct base relation:

```prolog
p(X, Z) :- finish(X, Z).
p(X, Z) :- step(X, Y), p(Y, Z).
```

`step(a, b)` and `finish(b, c)` give answer `c` to `p(a, Z)?`.
Neither the visited state `b` nor the seed `a` is an answer unless a
base derivation establishes it. Replacing this program by forward closure
over `step`, or reversing its recursive join, would not preserve `R* B`.

### Several origins and growing demand

For `start(S), path(S, T)?`, each distinct requested `S` enters the same
magic relation. Propagation retains `S` in the origin column, and the
answer is paired with that `S`. If two origins reach a shared state,
their walks contain two tuples there. Deduplicating those tuples by current
state alone would mix their answers.

No call-site tag is needed: equal bound tuples ask the same extensional
question about a fixed predicate. Callers can impose further constraints
on outputs through the ordinary query terms and joins. Different bound
tuples, including vectors that agree in only some columns, stay distinct.

Requests can also be produced by other generated rules as evaluation
proceeds. The rewrite does not snapshot the seed relation or impose a
handwritten walk-then-answer schedule. Dependency analysis can place seeds,
walks, answers and callers in the same positive component when required.
Semi-naive evaluation propagates each newly requested origin in that
component under the existing delta contracts.

## Least-fixed-point equivalence

Fix the complete inputs and an eligible adornment. Existentially project
each recursive rule's positive conjunction onto its current and successor
states and union the results to obtain `R`. Union the base rule relations
and source facts to obtain `B`. Output separation makes the source
operator exactly `F(P) = B union R P`. Its least fixed point is
`P = union(k >= 0, R^k B)`.

For a fixed requested set `Q`, the walk rules have least solution:

```text
D0 = { (a, a) : a in Q }
D  = D0 R* = { (a, s) : a in Q and R*(a, s) }
A  = D B   = { (a, y) : a in Q and P(a, y) }
```

**Soundness.** A generated walk tuple has a finite sequence of source
transitions from its recorded origin. A generated answer combines it with
a source base derivation. Applying those recursive source rules above the
base derivation proves the corresponding original `p` tuple. Repeated
states, cycles and multiple base rules do not change that argument.

**Completeness.** A finite derivation of an original `p(a, y)` consists of
zero or more certified recursive steps followed by a base derivation.
The walk seed establishes `(a, a)`, each step establishes the next walk
tuple, and the base rule then establishes `(a, y)` in the adorned answer.
The unchanged output tuple can be passed through every source step because
the certificate proves that no transition constrains it.

The equality holds for every `Q`, and both constructions are monotone in
`Q`. If callers generate requests recursively, each finite answer proof
uses finitely many earlier requests; the same argument applies as those
requests arise. Thus the factored predicate supplies the same answers to
each caller in the surrounding positive fixed point. Ordinary Magic's
soundness and completeness argument applies to the other adornments.

The proof also covers a fully bound call: the output vector is empty,
and a base derivation proves that the original requested tuple is true.
It does not license mixing origins, permuting output columns, changing
base rules, or factoring just some recursive rules of one adornment.

## Integration with the Magic search

The existing complete-set analysis and materialization run first. The
recognizer uses that fixed set; it does not request another materialization
pass. Source rules are found through `rules_by_head`, and component identity
comes from the original schedule already mapped by the rewrite.

When a search visits a kept bound adornment, it obtains a charged analysis
record for that source and mask. The record contains either ordinary
planner walks or a factoring certificate. A certificate records the column
partition, the base/transition role of each source rule, the recursive
literal position for a transition, and the checked construction bounds.
It owns no data-sized state or runtime bindings.

A certified adornment has no outgoing demanded calls in its transformed
definition: its external atoms are complete and its one self-call becomes
a walk transition. The search therefore does not run ordinary SIPS on
that removed call. Doing so could discover a weaker or free self-adornment
that only the unfactored nesting needed, recreating the closure this
transformation avoids. Ineligible adornments follow the ordinary planner
walk and call-discovery path unchanged.

Recognition commits its decision only after every source rule passes.
If a later rule fails, no calls from earlier rules have been suppressed
in a published analysis record; obtain the ordinary walks for the whole
adornment. Work already spent remains charged. Eligibility is not a flag
that the draft can override for an individual source rule.

### Free-call subsumption

Free calls keep their existing precedence. If any reachable consumer
requires `p` free, the final search retains `p$ff` and redirects bound
calls to it. It emits neither a factored `p$alpha` nor its walk predicate.
The free adornment follows ordinary source-rule discovery.

The search still needs at most its existing two passes. A record's
ordinary walks or certificate depend only on the source, mask and fixed
complete set, not on free marks. A certified record has no outgoing calls;
an ordinary record has the same outgoing calls whenever it is revisited.
The second search redirects according to the first search's fixed free
marks, so it cannot discover a free source the first search did not reach.
Store records with the existing reusable search analysis, not in a third
independent discovery pass.

Predicates classified complete before search bypass factoring altogether.
Their original rules are evaluated from the original schedule, preserving
whole-relation consumers, negation and aggregate groups.

## Drafting, naming and ownership

Draft only the final kept adornments. Keep the existing query-generated
seed rules first. For each factored adornment, emit the walk seed, the
virtual base-store answer rule when present, and then one generated rule
per source rule in program order. A recursive source rule emits a
transition; a base rule emits an answer. Preserve the order of copied
source atoms after removing the recursive atom and prepending the walk.
The ordinary planner determines their execution order.

Name the walk predicate `walk$` followed by the ordinary adorned name,
for example `walk$path$bf`. Its arity is twice the mask's population count.
Keep the ordinary adorned and magic names and arities. Name those first,
then the walk, in retained-adornment order. Names contain no seed values,
allocation addresses, hash values or task counters. The reserved `$`
namespace separates them from program identifiers.

The record distinguishes a factored adornment from an ordinary one; a
factored record owns a valid walk identity only after naming succeeds.
It is never inferred from a predicate's spelling. Explain retains the
mode and generated identities; construction-only slot maps and certificates
are released with the search arena when rewriting returns.

### Safe slot construction

`canonicalize()` in `lib/magic.c` copies variable metadata through the
draft's source rule. Fresh origin slots therefore need a scratch source
view with a valid variable table; assigning a larger slot number in copied
terms alone would index beyond the original table.

Build that scratch view with the preflighted compact source slots and
fresh origin slots. Preserve source locations and origin attribution.
Origin slots use immutable static display spellings `Origin$0`, `Origin$1`
and so on, in bound-column order. The `$` makes them disjoint from source
variable names, and static storage makes their bytes outlive every draft
and rewrite. Copied source names retain their existing source-owned
lifetime. This distinction matters because `canonicalize()` copies the
variable record, including `name.bytes`, without copying the name's bytes.

All generated terms refer to the scratch view until ordinary
canonicalization copies and renumbers them. Recompute occurrences through
that existing canonicalizer so liveness and existence-check planning see
the generated rule's actual uses. No display-name allocation has to survive
a dropped draft or the scratch reset.

Source rule storage is immutable. Draft heads, bodies and variable metadata
live in scratch until `publish_rule()` copies the canonical rule into the
rewrite's kept arena. It performs the existing structural comparison and
drops duplicates or rules that derive nothing. Neither a kept rule nor
explain metadata can retain a pointer into the reset scratch or search arena.

| Owner | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| `lib/magic.c` | Certify the partition, integrate discovery and subsumption, draft rules through the existing charged publication path, and own temporary analysis |
| Magic records in `lib/internal.h` | Represent ordinary versus factored analysis and preserve only the mode and generated identities needed after rewriting |
| `lib/explain.c` | Mark a retained factored adornment and print its ordinary generated rules under the established formatting contract |
| `lib/stratify.c`, `lib/plan.c`, `lib/eval.c` | Analyze, plan and evaluate the generated rules through their ordinary interfaces |
| `include/relic.h`, CLI strategy parsing and manuals | Expose the reference control consistently without changing configuration layout |

## Failure paths and limits

Factoring uses the same `max_magic` work counter and charged arenas as
ordinary Magic. Charge each source body before inspecting it for a
certificate, including a rejected attempt. Charge ordinary walks separately
when rejection requires them, and charge analysis replay in a second search
as ordinary walks are charged. Source arity and slot counts have fixed
language bounds, so term inspection is bounded by the charged body work.

Charge every generated draft before canonicalizing or deduplicating it,
including the walk seed, and charge each generated name through the
ordinary naming funnel. A factored adornment creates at most three named
predicates: adorned answers, magic requests and walks. Each extra walk
predicate has a charged seed rule, so it cannot grow independently of
metered work. Facts use one base-view rule regardless of fact count.

Certificate arrays follow source-rule count; slot maps follow the fixed
variable bound. Array lengths, name lengths and allocation sizes use
checked arithmetic. Do not raise the default work ceiling merely to conceal
unmetered recognition or copying. The default remains a function of the
original program and query, independent of their data or number of origins.

Outcomes follow the existing boundaries:

| Outcome | Action |
| --- | --- |
| No certificate, including representation bounds | Ordinary Magic for the entire adornment; no warning |
| Rewrite work limit exceeded during recognition, naming or drafting | Abandon the whole rewrite with its existing warning and answer from the original program |
| Allocation or accounted-memory refusal | Fail through the ordinary diagnostic and cleanup path; no silent retry under another optimization policy |
| Successful draft | Analyze the complete generated rule table before evaluating any of it |

Generated dependencies are positive; complete external reads add no new
strict edge. The existing stratification check still validates the entire
rewrite. A compiler-internal inconsistency is an internal failure, not an
unsupported user program.

Partially drafted rules are private to the query rewrite. Reset releases
their arenas and metadata; the evaluator sees no partial factored family.
Generated predicate identities and already interned symbols can persist
under the existing context-lifetime contract, but a later query reuses
neither derived walk tuples nor source-analysis records. Discard and
query cleanup keep their existing ownership boundaries.

All generated relations, including origin columns, count toward tuple and
memory limits. Fewer derivations can change where a limit is reached
relative to an unfactored strategy. Within one strategy, repeated runs and
thread counts retain the existing determinism and failure contracts.

## Determinism and reference control

Recognition reads syntax, original component membership, a bound mask and
the fixed complete set. Rule traversal, naming, column projection and fresh
slot construction use source and column order. Hash tables find equal
records; their bucket order never determines generated order.

The flag `RELIC_F_NO_FACTOR`, exposed as `-X no-factor`, disables this
transformation while retaining ordinary Magic, including its subsumption
and supplementary rules. `RELIC_F_NO_MAGIC` bypasses both; an already
materialized context also uses its established direct query path.
`-X naive -X no-magic` remains the whole-program semantic reference on
small inputs. Other strategy flags govern the generated rules normally.

The public flag occupies an available configuration bit and does not add
a field to `relic_config_t`. CLI help, manuals, flag validation and strategy
tests describe the same control. The implementation plan tracks those
changes together with the rewrite.

Explain marks a retained factored adornment as
`adorn path/2 bf, factored`; the line grammar belongs to
[planning](design-planning.md#explain). Walks are helper predicates, not
separate adornments. Generated rules and their plans use normal component
output. A source redirected to a free adornment shows ordinary subsumption
instead. Attribution of walk storage, plans and rules names the source
predicate in diagnostics; explain shows generated names.

Generated execution uses the same serial and shared-round contracts, so
plans, unsorted results and counters agree across thread counts, except
the counters explicitly about parallel rounds. Different optimization
strategies need equal answer sets, not equal derivation order, identifiers,
resource use or explain text.

## Work and storage bounds

For a requested origin `a`, let `V_a` be its reached state set, `E_a` the
transition matches expanded from those states, and `B_a` the base matches
at those states. The walk relation holds exactly the union of
`{a} x V_a`, and the answer relation holds only requested-origin answers.
It holds no answers for intermediate states merely because the walk visits
them. With several origins, bounds sum over origins; the required
correlation can itself require many tuples.

For one seed, one binary transition relation and one binary base relation,
an indexed delta-driven plan visits `O(|V_a| + |E_a| + |B_a|)` rows and
stores `O(|V_a| + |answers_a|)` generated tuples. Inputs and their indexes
are additional ordinary storage. For a chain, the generated relation sizes
are linear in chain length, whereas unfactored demanded answers can hold
every suffix's paths.

The relation-size property follows from the rewrite regardless of join
order. The row-visit bound also requires suitable access paths and driver
selection; counter tests verify that the ordinary planner achieves it on
the targeted graph shapes. It is not a claim that arbitrary conjunctive
transition bodies or skewed joins run in time proportional to their
distinct projected pairs. Those bodies can perform substantial join work,
and their complete inputs can already be expensive to materialize.

The origin columns cost `m` extra domain words per walk tuple compared
with a singleton-specific representation. They buy one correlation rule
for constant seeds, multiple seeds and requests discovered during recursion.
No origin dictionary, surrogate identifiers or per-seed generated program
is needed.

## Validation and acceptance

Write a failing work-counter regression before implementing the rewrite.
A chain of `N = 1000` edges with right-recursive `path` and a query from
its first node gives `N` answers. Require the default query to derive at
most `8N + 64` tuples, a generous bound for requests, walks, answers and
query output. The unfactored demanded closure contains `N(N + 1) / 2`
path tuples, so the existing path fails the bound for the intended reason.
Also inspect that the final rewrite actually contains a factored adornment.
After the reference flag exists, require the same answer set under
`no-factor` while keeping its larger work as a negative control.

Use the existing graph equivalence and rewrite inspection infrastructure
in `tests/unit/test_magic.c`, language/explain cases, `tests/perf.sh`, and
the allocation-failure harness in `tests/unit/test_exhaustion.c`.

| Concern | Required evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Fixed-point equivalence | Independent small random `R` and `B`, unions of several recursive and base rules, and base facts agree with ordinary Magic and naive whole-program evaluation |
| Positive length | Isolated origins, empty bases, self-loops and cycles return the origin only when a source base derivation and transitions justify it |
| Correlation | Several starts with disjoint and overlapping reachable sets, vector states, repeated requests and multi-call joins never exchange answers between origins |
| Growing requests | A recursive caller generates new seeds from earlier factored answers; the joint fixed point agrees with the unfactored program |
| Column mapping | Nonleading bound columns, fully bound calls, state constants and repeated state terms, base-head constants and repeated outputs preserve original positions and equality |
| Certificate rejection | One violating rule among otherwise valid rules, changed or observed recursive outputs, nonlinear or mutual recursion, computed values, strict reads and demanded external predicates all retain ordinary behavior |
| Subsumption | A later free call removes an earlier factored candidate; both searches reuse the correct analysis, and only the free copy is drafted |
| Complete reads | Positive calls alongside negation or aggregation read the existing complete source; a materialized context skips rewriting |
| Structural limits | `2m` at and beyond the arity limit, source slot counts around the fresh-origin bound, and canonicalization at slot 63 never form an out-of-range shift or array access |
| Work limit | Recognition, rejection, replay, long names, discarded drafts and the extra seed count toward one budget; exact-boundary tests exercise success and whole-program fallback |
| Exhaustion and reuse | Failure at each certificate, draft and naming allocation cleans up; a failed or abandoned query followed by another query, materialization or load retains no stale walk or analysis state |
| Determinism | Repeated runs, hash seeds and 1, 2, 4 and 16 workers agree on the strategy's plans and unsorted rows; forced serial and deferred execution agree |
| Performance scope | Chain and bound acyclic/cyclic closure avoid unrequested suffix answers; unsupported and already efficient left-recursive controls remain competitive |

Extend the strategy matrix with `no-factor` so the reference remains
independently exercised after default explain goldens change. Compare sorted
sets between strategies and unsorted streams within a strategy. A flag
combination must not accidentally make both sides use the same rewrite.
Tests specifically about ordinary SIPS, supplementary cuts or their exact
work charges select `no-factor`; factoring has its own structural and
charging tests. Updating default goldens does not replace either check.

Run the required code, security and readability review before implementation
tests, then the full suite and ASan/UBSan for arena ownership, slot masks
and size arithmetic. Exercise threaded evaluation, and run TSan for any
changes to shared execution paths. LSan and MSan run where supported.

Benchmark `chain-right`, `tc-acyclic-bf` and `tc-cyclic-bf` at scales 1, 2,
4 and 8 with one and sixteen threads under the
[measurement rules](../docs/todo-benchmark-performance.md#measurement-and-acceptance-rules).
Record derivations, generated relation sizes, persistent index storage,
rewrite/planning time, evaluation time, wall time and peak RSS. Include
different `R` and `B`, multiple origins and ineligible programs as controls.
The mechanism is accepted when requested-origin work replaces suffix
closure materialization, exact sets agree, and the counter regression
passes. A repeatable unexplained regression on the controls blocks default
enablement; exploratory reachability timings are not a promised speedup.

## Alternatives not taken

* **Reverse every right-recursive rule.** `R* B` need not equal `B R*`.
  The state/output certificate justifies propagation through `R` followed
  by `B`, without an algebraic assumption about their equality.
* **Union all reached states and pair their answers with all seeds.**
  This loses which origin can reach which base match. Carrying the origin
  makes correlation part of every tuple's identity.
* **Drop origin columns when one seed appears likely.** Caller bindings
  can grow during evaluation. One representation avoids a cardinality
  guess, a representation switch and separate proofs for singleton calls.
* **Only change the recursive join order.** A better plan can reduce work
  per derivation while still materializing every demanded suffix answer.
* **A special graph traversal executor.** Ordinary generated rules retain
  the engine's set semantics, limits, determinism and ownership contracts.
* **Factor only the recursive rules that match.** Another source rule
  can observe or change the output, invalidating the predicate equation.
* **Probe data to prove eligibility or materialize more dependencies.**
  Structural separation gives a query-independent semantic proof; extra
  scans or complete relations can consume the benefit before evaluation.
