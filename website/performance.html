Methods and references

  • Semi-naive evaluation. Delta relations avoid repeating joins made entirely from old facts. Method reference: Abiteboul, Hull and Vianu, Foundations of Databases, chapter 13. Implementation.
  • Magic Sets. Query bindings become demand predicates that restrict bottom-up evaluation. Beeri and Ramakrishnan, On the Power of Magic (1987). Implementation.
  • Bound recursive query factoring. Eligible rewrites reduce the columns carried through recursion, with checks to preserve correlations between origins. Naughton, Ramakrishnan, Sagiv and Ullman, Efficient Evaluation of Right-, Left-, and Multi-Linear Rules (1989). Implementation.
  • Indexed joins and existence checks. A greedy planner uses bindings, relation sizes and key counts; secondary indexes cover bound columns. Local planning design.
  • Interned values and append-only relations. Tuples hold compact identifiers, and hash indexes provide membership and lookup paths. Values and storage.
  • Parallel rounds with ordered publication. Workers read frozen views; candidate publication preserves serial identifiers, answers and diagnostics. Local parallel design.
  • Grouped count destinations. Eligible count queries deliver completed keys and counts without constructing a second answer relation. Local aggregation design.
  • Thompson regular-expression simulation. Explicit instruction-thread lists keep matching linear in input length for a fixed pattern. Thompson, Regular Expression Search Algorithm (1968). Expression implementation.
